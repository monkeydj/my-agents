# User-Centered Design

> Involve affected users and understand their needs, behaviors, and perspectives at every phase of problem-solving and solution design.

---

## Core Idea

Users reveal the real problem and validate real solutions. Don't assume; go ask.

---

## Why It Matters

- **Assumptions mislead** — what we *think* users need often misses what they *actually* need
- **Hidden needs** — users sometimes can't articulate needs until shown prototypes
- **Fitness to purpose** — only users can say if a solution actually works in their context
- **Buy-in and adoption** — users involved in design feel ownership

---

## How to Apply

### Discovery Phase
- Interview users about their needs, behaviors, routines
- Observe users in their actual context
- Ask "why" to understand underlying motivations, not just stated preferences
- Don't frame questions in terms of solutions ("which X?") — ask about needs ("what problem do you face?")

### Development Phase
- Prototype early and test with users
- Gather feedback on prototypes
- Iterate based on user feedback
- Fail fast with users, not after launch

### Delivery Phase
- Conduct rigorous testing in real-world conditions
- Continue gathering feedback post-launch
- Evaluate whether solution solved the actual problem
